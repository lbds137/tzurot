### Theme: Character voice drift in long conversations

_Focus: a character that has talked for months should sound like its card, not like a register that grew in its own history._

**Owner intake 2026-09-05, HIGH priority, the entry point for re-opening the memory overhaul (`doc-8`) as the beta.219 active epic.** Owner's words: none of the other work matters if they feel like they are losing touch with their characters. Backfill is back on the table. Model-parameter changes (thinking level, temperature) are ruled out unless absolutely necessary. beta.218 cuts first.

**Evidence** (owner handoff and debug payload under `docs/local/handoffs/`, gitignored; the drift curve from `voice-drift-curve.ts`, stored under `docs/local/handoffs/` and copied into `scripts/analysis/` to run because workspace packages only resolve from there, run read-only against prod on 2026-09-05, aggregates only):

- The prompt at generation time: ~7.6k tokens of card and protocol in the cached prefix, then ~30k tokens of cross-channel history (half of it the character's own turns), ~12k of channel history, then a ~13k-token memory archive and the current turn in the uncached tail. `cachedPromptTokens` 36,864 of 51,723. The card sits ~45k tokens upstream of the generation point behind the cache boundary, which is a legitimate constraint (prefix-cache hits) that the fix must preserve.
- Per-month stats over the character's memories (assistant side, per 1k chars): exclamation marks ran 1.3–3.8 from 2025-11 through 2026-02, 0.7–1.0 from April to July, then **0.06 in August and 0.01 in September**. The courtroom vocabulary (ledger, verdict, exhibit, archive, filed, ruling) is **zero through July**, then appears in August and doubles or triples in September. A pet name absent from the card runs 0.01 in April, 0.26 in August, 0.53 in September. Average reply length 830 chars in April, 1,652 in September. So this is a slow fade through spring and a **cliff in August 2026**, not a smooth five-month slide. August also carries 529 memories, the heaviest month on record, so the archive's self-reinforcement loop (each retrieval carries the character's own drifted prose verbatim) had the most material exactly when the register crystallised.
- The cross-channel history feature shipped 2026-02-28, so it is not the August trigger by itself. Candidates for what changed in August, unverified: usage volume (the archive loop), a model or preset change on the character (the current preset is `glm-5.3` at temperature 1.0), or both. Check the preset's history before attributing.
- Memory rows store `{user}: …\n{assistant}: …` with the reply verbatim (`services/ai-worker/src/services/LongTermMemoryService.ts` `storeInteraction`); the archive renders each as a `<historical_note>` (`services/ai-worker/src/services/prompt/MemoryFormatter.ts` `formatSingleMemory`). The protocol directives (`Structural Variety`, `Response Length Calibration`, `System Prompt Primacy`) live in the database `characterDirectives` JSON, rendered by `PersonalityFieldsFormatter.ts`; none of the three phrases appears in code.

### Phase 4 — the history-side treatment (OPENED 2026-09-13 by the fresh-thread probe; design pass, beta.225 horizon)

_Focus: keep the continuity cross-channel history provides without shipping 70k tokens of the character's own drifted prose to the generation point._

- [ ] The design question: the probe above shows the register survives with history off and collapses with it on, while the archive (user side verbatim, character side summarised) is register-neutral. The candidate shape is the same treatment for cross-channel history that `doc-8` settled for the archive — the user side verbatim, the character side reduced (summary, or the archive tier's own render) — so the continuity the out-of-scope entry below protects is kept and the reinforcement mass is not. Counter-candidates to weigh at the pass: a per-character cap on the character-side share of the block; rendering cross-channel turns as facts-plus-user-side only. Discriminating probe before the pass: the same fresh thread with cross-channel history capped at ~20 messages — register held = mass is the variable and a cap is a candidate; collapsed = the block's mere presence is, and only a re-render works.
- [x] **Stop-gap RULED 2026-09-13**: cross-channel history stays ON for Emily and the drifted voice is accepted until this pass investigates other fixes. The owner's reason is the design constraint for the pass: cross-channel history carries recent life details the character otherwise lacks, while memory pulls are the weaker channel for that — random at times, and prone to resurfacing states that have ended (TASK-671, third sighting of its second instance recorded there). So the value to keep is the USER side of the block, the cost to shed is the CHARACTER side, which is the shape the candidate above already has; any pass that trades away the user side is off the table.
- [ ] **Candidate design, grounded 2026-09-13 (not yet a spec)**: the summaries already exist — `memories.assistant_summary` (with `summary_status`, `source_content_hash`, `summary_prompt_version`) is the archive summarizer's output, council-reviewed under `doc-8` D2 and populated by the pre-warm; a second summary column on `conversation_history` would duplicate it. The cross-channel block (`services/ai-worker/src/services/context/CrossChannelSerializer.ts`) would render user turns verbatim and each assistant turn from the summary of the memory that turn belongs to, verbatim fallback where no summary exists; the current channel stays verbatim. The one real design detail is the join: `memories.message_ids` holds the TRIGGER (user) message id only (`LongTermMemoryService.ts` `storeInteraction`), so an assistant row maps through the user row before it in the same channel; a one-line additive producer change (also record the reply id) makes the join direct going forward, and the sequence assumption needs checking against real rows before anything builds on it. Gate it behind a sibling per-character setting rather than the archive render switch, so the two flips measure separately. Cost: no new summarizer spend for characters on the archive switch; the block shrinks (run A: 71.5k tokens, replies averaging 1.6k chars, summaries a fraction). Pre-pass gates: (1) coverage — of the assistant turns in a recent cross-channel window, how many have a memory row with a current summary (read-only prod query; owner runs it); (2) the honest A/B — replay run A's exact prompt on dev with the summaries substituted, one generation. Owner constraint from the ruling above: the semantics of what the character said must survive; naive dropping of the assistant side is out.
- [ ] **Observation 2026-09-13 (owner, n=1, unverified)**: after un-drifting the register in a channel with cross-channel history OFF, turning it back ON kept the fixed register in that channel. Reading: the channel's own recent turns sit nearest the generation point and are the strongest style reference; the cross-channel block is upstream of them. Suggests a cheap lever worth a measurement — seeding a channel with a few on-register turns — and predicts the drift returns in a channel whose own history is drifted.
- [ ] **Measured cost of holding the register (2026-09-13, one prod-shaped dev turn, `glm-5.3`)**: with the cross-channel feed back (100 messages, 75k history tokens, 93k prompt), the reply was ~280 tokens but completion was 4,411 and the model call took 72.5 s — the reasoning trace (18.5k chars, 51 paragraphs, no loops, no confusion markers) spends most of its length policing the register: flagging "interrogating" and "answer for" as courtroom-adjacent and rewriting them, choosing "more exclamatory, more Emily-bubbly", checking emoji reuse, calibrating length against the recent turns, and explicitly steering away from drifted vocabulary it can see in the thread history. So the anchor and the Voice Fidelity directive work, and their per-turn price while the drifted mass is in the prompt is ~4k reasoning tokens and most of a minute. Prediction for the pass: shrinking the mass (the digest, the summary join) shrinks the deliberation; measure completion tokens and latency before/after as the cheap proxy. Also observed the same day: a stored relationship fact lost to similarity retrieval on one turn and returned the next (TASK-964), and a channel purge silently emptied the feed through the persona-wide epoch (TASK-966).
- [ ] **Design brief for the pass (owner brainstorm 2026-09-13, distilled; the premise it rests on was never recorded before)**. _The premise_: the owner runs cross-channel history as a CONTINUITY FEED — a rolling ~100-message window of the week across channels so the character knows what happened recently ("I talk to her every day") — and it papers over the memory system's gap, because nothing in memory serves that need: extraction drops non-durable events by design ("would this still be true in six months"), and the archive is similarity-retrieved, so the week surfaces only when the current message resembles it. The cost of the feed is the register mass (this theme). _The second limitation_: every layer selects by similarity to the current message; nothing renders because of what it IS. A stored relationship fact lost to ten closer neighbours (TASK-964 is the minimal fix: reserved slots for locked, corrected, and relationship-tagged facts). _Candidates for the pass, in the order to try them_: (1) a deterministic **standing-context block** picked per CATEGORY rather than per query — facts already carry `kind:name` entity tags, so the index exists implicitly; render relationship + forms of address, current life state, standing commitments, a few preferences, each with a small cap chosen by salience and recency inside the category; fixed budget of a few hundred tokens, no model call, no latency; a fixed category list keeps it "not too loose". (2) a **recent-days digest** per persona × character — a rolling third-person note of the user's last ~7 days across channels, regenerated by a background job (the archive summarizer's machinery pointed at a time window, not a memory), a few hundred tokens, register-neutral by construction, indifferent to provider latency because it never runs on the reply path; it takes over the continuity role, and cross-channel history shrinks to a small recent tail or turns off. (3) **agentic drill-down** (the character retrieves on demand through a tool, the index-then-open pattern): deferred with the reason measured, not assumed — the reply path already spends 14–35 s in the model call on the two 2026-09-13 payloads, and a retrieve-then-answer loop roughly doubles that plus tokens; the deterministic index yields the categories such a layer would drill into if it is ever built, so it is the prerequisite either way. Net shape: tiers by TIME — durable facts (exist) · recent-days digest (new, the continuity feed) · verbatim archive for specifics (exist) — with the category-indexed standing block on top for relationship management. The earlier summary-join candidate above remains the fallback if the digest cannot carry the continuity the owner relies on.
